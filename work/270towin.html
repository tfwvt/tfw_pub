Case study · 270toWin

Election maps that move with the market.

270toWin is one of the web’s go-to places to build your own electoral map. For the 2026 midterm cycle we helped bring live prediction-market data from Kalshi into the site, alongside the interactive maps and polling tools readers rely on.

Client
270toWin
Partner data
Kalshi prediction markets
Services
Front-end development, data visualization, API integration
Timeline
Ongoing, 2016 – present
The challenge

Make fast-moving numbers easy to read.

Election readers want two things at once: the big picture and the detail behind it. Prediction markets add a third layer — prices that change minute to minute.

The goal was to present that data clearly and responsibly, in 270toWin’s familiar style, without slowing down pages that see heavy traffic around every election. Each visual needed to work on a phone, update on its own, and sit comfortably next to the maps and polling averages readers already know.

What we built

Four new ways to read an election.

Live candidate treemaps

Each candidate’s tile is sized by the probability implied by Kalshi’s latest trading price, so the frontrunners literally take up more space. Treemaps for the 2028 presidential winner and both parties’ nominees can run side by side — three live visuals on one page, each updating independently.

  • Probability-weighted layouts with candidate photos
  • Party-colored tiles and clear percentage labels
  • Combined “everyone else” bucket keeps small fields readable

Senate, House & Governor maps

The interactive maps now show where the market thinks each chamber is heading. Readers can switch between the current Congress, a consensus forecast and the market view, then build and share their own forecast.

  • Market odds for chamber control alongside the map
  • Custom map color palettes and a map library
  • One-click share and embed for social and publishers

Polling at a glance

Polling averages for every state render as a sortable grid of pie charts, with the leader and margin called out beneath each. Race pages pair the current market odds with a full table of polls, so readers can compare the money with the surveys.

  • Sort by date, state, competitiveness or margin
  • Per-race poll tables with averages highlighted
  • Market odds embedded directly on race pages

Odds-spread analysis

A dumbbell chart lines up each competitive House district and shows the gap between Kalshi’s price and an independent forecaster’s rating. The biggest disagreements jump straight out — exactly the kind of story a table hides.

More from the build

Across the whole site.

The result

Richer election coverage, same familiar site.

  • Prediction-market data presented next to polls and forecasts, with clear sourcing and timestamps
  • New visual formats — treemaps and dumbbell charts — that work on phones as well as desktops
  • Market odds integrated across President, Senate, House and Governor content
  • A long-running partnership that has grown with the site since its classic interactive map
